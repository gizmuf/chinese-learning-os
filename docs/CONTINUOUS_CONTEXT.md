# Reading context across dictionary, Atlas and writing

This increment extends the existing v71 application; it does not replace the Atlas, notebook, scheduler or lesson corpus. Experimental reader/map A/B files remain outside the application repository.

The dictionary carries an authored `DictionaryContext` (text, pinyin, meaning, optional source ID and exact sentence recording). A selected polyphonic reading removes a canonical word identity and isolated recording when they describe a different reading. A normal nested lookup keeps its source; a separately selected example establishes its own authored source. My Day sentence 6 keeps 得 **děi** and the breakfast/school sentence throughout writing and contextual Atlas.

`SharedWriting` receives the whole source example, not merely a header override. A supplied reviewed item is matched by its exact ID, spelling and reading; it is never replaced with the first item sharing its spelling. Source-specific or unregistered senses save supported writing drafts, not a different curriculum item's grade. Existing independent review rules remain unchanged. Guided animation completion also requires successfully loaded stroke data; a failed load cannot produce a complete caption or an enabled Save action, and has a real retry control. The unrelated degree-complement example 她说得很好 retains its ID and wording, with the displayed reading corrected to neutral **de**, also retained in its authoring override. No recordings were regenerated.

Ordinary dictionary exploration opens the real Atlas in a modal over the mounted reader. The original sentence and its current reading remain available. A separate writing modal leaves the map mounted. Back restores the definition, and closing that definition returns to the same source without opening another browser tab. Curated single-character word-kind routes such as 电 retain their source just as extended character routes do; deliberate different word readings remain distinct senses.

Tab-local `sessionStorage` checkpoints contain navigation/display state only. Context checkpoints are scoped to owner, locale and original URL; My Day layers, grouping, focus index and scroll have an owner-scoped view checkpoint. Source IDs are checked against current canonical My Day, Shuoshuo or localized grammar-example rows. Unknown or stale source snapshots and malformed data are rejected safely. Canonical word references are rehydrated from existing vocabulary; no learner database is copied or migrated. Closing an exploration clears its active checkpoint. The browser Back button closes the exploration branch; the visible Previous connection button traverses its bounded route trail.

My Day meaning groups reconstruct all 26 original sentences exactly. Nine explicit My Day/Shuoshuo construction occurrences link to existing grammar frames and examples. Lexical word taps and construction actions remain separate. Full source notes, original wording, uncertainty and the absence of exact Shuoshuo sentence audio remain accessible.

Word-family lists show existing authored readings/meanings first. Broader CC-CEDICT spellings are fetched only for the visible page, with at most three concurrent requests across changing pages, success caching and stale-view guards. Names, spelling variants, unknown entries and network failures remain distinct. Structural character counts and literal word counts are separate; no additional HSK exploration ceiling is introduced. All nine Atlas layouts remain available under Other views.

StudyStudio labels its four movement examples separately from the full My Day lesson and exposes five predefined actions without an arbitrary-question text box or a live-AI claim. No new provider, model, credential, paid service, storage binding or TTS call is introduced.

See `CONTINUOUS_CONTEXT_QA.md` for acceptance. Browser viewport testing does not constitute physical iPad, Pencil/palm-rejection or measured learning-outcome acceptance.
